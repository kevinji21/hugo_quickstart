## Summary

The Palo Alto City Council held a regular meeting on October 5, 2026, addressing a wide range of city business. Key discussions included best practices for managing city consultants, approving strategies to support local businesses, and advancing partnerships for senior services at the Cubberley site. The Council also made appointments to various city commissions, recognized Fire Prevention Week, and took positions on upcoming ballot measures.

Key actions and discussions included:
*   Appointed candidates to the Human Relations, Parks and Recreation, and Planning and Transportation Commissions.
*   Issued a proclamation recognizing Fire Prevention Week.
*   Discussed best practices for managing consultant services used by the city.
*   Approved a strategy to retain, expand, and attract local businesses.
*   Approved partnerships to expand services for aging adults at the Cubberley site.
*   Approved surveillance use policies for security cameras at the Animal Shelter and Junior Museum & Zoo.
*   Approved budget adjustments and a contract for railroad crossing safety improvements.
*   Approved work plans for several city commissions and boards.
*   Considered and took positions on state and local measures for the November 2026 ballot.

## Agenda Highlights

**1. Boards, Commissions, and Committees Appointments:** The City Council appointed new members to the Human Relations Commission (HRC), Parks and Recreation Commission (PRC), and Planning and Transportation Commission (PTC).

**2. Proclamation Recognizing Fire Prevention Week:** The Council issued a proclamation to officially recognize October 4-10 as Fire Prevention Week, highlighting the importance of fire safety.

**3. Study Session: Best Practices in Managing Consultant Services:** The Council held a discussion session to review and understand best practices for how the city manages the services provided by outside consultants. This was an informational discussion to improve efficiency and oversight.

**4. Approval of Business Retention, Expansion, and Attraction (BRE/A) Strategy:** The Council approved a strategy recommended by the Economic Development Committee aimed at supporting existing local businesses, helping them grow, and attracting new businesses to Palo Alto.

**5. Approval of Letters of Intent with Avenidas and HeartFit for Life:** The Council approved agreements to partner with Avenidas and HeartFit for Life. These partnerships will work towards expanding services for older adults at the Cubberley site.

**6. Approval of Surveillance Use Policy for Security Cameras at the Palo Alto Animal Shelter:** The Council approved a policy outlining the rules and guidelines for the use of security cameras at the Palo Alto Animal Shelter.

**7. Approval of Surveillance Use Policy for Security Cameras at the Palo Alto Junior Museum & Zoo:** The Council approved a policy establishing guidelines for the use of security cameras at the Palo Alto Junior Museum & Zoo.

**8. Approval of FY 2026 Reappropriation Requests to FY 2027 Operating and Capital Improvement Budgets:** The Council approved requests to move unspent funds from the 2026 fiscal year to the 2027 budget for both daily operations and larger capital improvement projects, along with other technical budget adjustments.

**9. Approval of Professional Services Contract with BKF Engineers for Quiet Zone Railroad Crossings:** The Council approved a contract with BKF Engineers for the design of new four-quadrant gate systems at the Churchill Avenue, Meadow Drive, and Charleston Road railroad crossings. These improvements are necessary for establishing "Quiet Zones" where train horns are not routinely sounded.

**10. Approval of FY 2027 Architectural Review Board, Historic Resources Board, Planning and Transportation Commission, and Utilities Advisory Commission Work Plans:** The Council approved the planned work agendas for the upcoming fiscal year for the Architectural Review Board, Historic Resources Board, Planning and Transportation Commission, and Utilities Advisory Commission.

**11. Consideration of City Council Positions on State and Local Measures:** The Council discussed and took official positions on various state and local ballot measures that will be presented to Palo Alto voters on the November 3, 2026, ballot.
